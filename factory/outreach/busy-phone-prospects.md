# Busy-Phone Problem: Prospect Directory & Outreach Playbook

## The "Cortese Busy-Phone Problem" Defined
Cortese Restaurant (Binghamton, NY; `607-723-6477`) exemplified the classic peak-hour bottleneck:
1. **Single phone ordering line**: During Friday–Sunday rushes (5:00 PM – 7:30 PM), staff is busy taking orders or seating guests.
2. **Busy signals & dropped calls**: Callers receive a busy tone or 6+ rings. Unlike a corporate office, hungry takeout callers do not leave voicemails—they hang up and dial the next restaurant or open DoorDash.
3. **Lost revenue**: 8–15 missed calls per weekend rush at an average ticket of $40–$55 = $320–$825/week ($1,300–$3,500/month) in lost orders.
4. **The Cortese Digital Solution**: Carrier-level busy/no-answer forward → caller presses 1 (or receives automated text) → SMS arrives with online menu link and tap-to-call back. Zero website changes, zero kitchen friction.

## 2. Prospect Discovery & Filtering Framework

To scale beyond Binghamton into any regional market (Syracuse, Rochester, Scranton, Albany), apply this 3-step filter:

### Step 1: Query Syntax
- **Google Maps / Yelp**: `[City] pizza takeout "call to order"`
- **Website Filter**: Find restaurant websites that feature a PDF menu or state *"Call [Phone] to place takeout orders"* with no Toast / Slice / ChowNow integration.
- **Review Mining**: Search reviews for:
  - `"phone was busy"`
  - `"couldn't get through"`
  - `"no one answered"`
  - `"off the hook"`

### Step 2: The 5:45 PM Friday Verification Call
Place a quick verification call to the prospect during their peak rush (Friday 5:45 PM – 6:15 PM):
- If you get a busy signal, call-waiting click, or voicemail, they are an **A-tier qualified prospect**.
- Log the timestamp and outcome into the CRM / prospect tracker.
