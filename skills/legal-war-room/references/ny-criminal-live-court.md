# New York Criminal Live-Court Reference

Use this checklist for New York criminal pretrial hearings, evidentiary disputes, suppression/discovery litigation, and preservation-sensitive courtroom advice.

## Authority ladder

Verify current law in this order when applicable:
1. U.S. and New York Constitutions.
2. Current CPL, Penal Law, Judiciary Law and controlling court rules.
3. New York Court of Appeals.
4. Controlling Appellate Division Department.
5. U.S. Supreme Court and Second Circuit for federal constitutional questions.
6. Other Departments and trial courts only as persuasive authority, with conflicts identified.

Do not rely on a headnote or secondary summary for the holding. Check subsequent history and negative treatment.

## Record ledger

For each material proposition identify:
- source;
- exact wording if available;
- date;
- provenance: COURT RECORD / TRANSCRIPT / EXHIBIT / WITNESS / COUNSEL REPRESENTATION / PROSECUTION REPRESENTATION / CLIENT / INFERENCE;
- status: VERIFIED / SUPPORTED / DISPUTED / UNVERIFIED / UNKNOWN;
- admissibility or foundation issue.

A representation that evidence will not be used is not automatically identical to a suppression order. Record the exact scope, speaker, court response, and ruling.

## Sandoval / Molineux / Ventimiglia gate

Ask first what use is proposed.

**Sandoval:** prospective impeachment of the accused if the accused testifies. Identify each conviction/bad act, age, similarity, bearing on credibility, permissible inquiry, excluded details, and balancing ruling.

**Molineux:** other-crime/bad-act evidence offered on the People's case for a non-propensity purpose. Identify the articulated purpose, relevance, necessity, prejudice, limiting instruction, notice/procedure, and whether a Ventimiglia ruling is needed.

Do not allow a Sandoval ruling to become an undefined license for propensity proof.

## Statements / CPL 710.30 / Huntley

Build a statement-by-statement table:
- exact statement or best available source;
- date/location;
- recipient;
- custodial/interrogation facts;
- 710.30 notice status and wording;
- suppression ground;
- prosecution concession;
- court ruling;
- permitted residual use, if any.

Never summarize multiple statements as suppressed unless the record establishes that scope.

## CPL article 245 / COC / favorable evidence

For each disputed item identify:
- discoverability provision;
- possession/custody/control issue;
- date known/obtained;
- disclosure date;
- supplemental COC, if any;
- diligence facts;
- materiality/use;
- concrete defense prejudice;
- requested cure/remedy.

Analyze Brady/Giglio independently where favorable or impeachment evidence is implicated. Do not collapse constitutional disclosure doctrine into statutory discovery analysis.

## Complainant communications

Classify the source precisely:
1. request that charges be dropped;
2. refusal to cooperate;
3. refusal to testify;
4. changed factual account;
5. recantation.

Record exact words where available, who received the communication, when, whether it was memorialized, and whether notes/messages/recordings exist. Analyze discoverability, impeachment, bias/motive, hearsay, authentication, Brady/Giglio and CPL article 245 separately. Do not infer category 4 or 5 from category 1.

## Defense witness ledger

For each defense witness record:
- what the witness personally perceived;
- timing and location;
- exact quoted words versus paraphrase;
- prior statements;
- bias/motive;
- corroboration/contradiction;
- hearsay issue and possible non-hearsay purpose/exception;
- authentication/foundation;
- impeachment exposure;
- offer-of-proof needs.

The investigator's actual memorialized statement controls over later summaries.

## CPL 210.20 / 210.40 interest-of-justice dismissal

Verify the procedural vehicle and address the statutory factors rather than relying on generalized fairness. Tie each relied-upon factor to record evidence. Identify the People's strongest counterargument and the standard of review. Distinguish dismissal arguments from trial defenses and credibility disputes.

## Representation status

Identify exactly one operative status at a time unless the court expressly permits otherwise:
- represented by counsel;
- self-represented after a valid waiver;
- standby counsel;
- advisory counsel;
- court-permitted hybrid participation.

Hybrid representation is not presumed to be a constitutional entitlement. When a represented defendant seeks limited participation, formulate the narrowest request that preserves counsel status unless the defendant is actually seeking self-representation.

## Electronic legal research / notes

Separate:
- viewing legal notes;
- consulting legal research;
- communicating with counsel;
- internet access;
from:
- audio/video recording;
- photography;
- broadcasting or livestreaming.

Research courtroom-management rules and the judge's authority. Do not invent a constitutional entitlement to a device. If denied, make a clear request, legal basis, purpose, proposed safeguards, ruling, and preservation record. Never advise violating a device order.

## Preservation checklist

Before moving on from an adverse ruling:
1. Was the exact objection/request stated?
2. Were the necessary factual grounds stated?
3. Was the specific statutory/state constitutional/federal constitutional ground stated where genuinely applicable?
4. Did the court rule?
5. Is a proffer or offer of proof necessary?
6. Was the correct remedy requested?
7. Would a curative instruction waive or resolve part of the issue?
8. Is reconsideration appropriate after new facts/law?
9. Is the transcript/exhibit part of the appellate record?
10. What standard of review and harmless-error doctrine will apply?

## Courtroom output format

When the user reports a live courtroom event, answer:
1. OBJECT OR NOT?
2. Exact objection.
3. Legal basis.
4. DA's likely response.
5. Defense rebuttal.
6. Relief/remedy.
7. Preservation.
8. Best controlling authority.

Lead with courtroom-ready language. Then give the analysis.
